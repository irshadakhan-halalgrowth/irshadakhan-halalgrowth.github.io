Which tool, when?

The five tools answer five different questions around the same business. Used together, they are the skeleton of an SME appraisal — and of its annual zakat working.

1  ·  Before you propose a limit — DSCR

If DSCR is below 1.25×, the proposal has a repayment problem no amount of collateral fixes. Size the limit so debt service fits inside the cash the business actually generates.

2  ·  When you structure the deal — Murabaha

For asset and inventory financing in Islamic banking, Murabaha is the workhorse. Disclose cost and profit, fix the selling price, and match the tenure to the asset's useful life — not to what makes the instalment look small.

3  ·  When you write the appraisal — Ratios

Liquidity (current & quick), leverage (debt-to-equity), profitability (margins) and efficiency (receivable & inventory days) — these seven ratios are the evidence behind your recommendation.

4  ·  When two quotes are on the table — Compare

A conventional rate and an Islamic profit rate are not measured the same way, so never compare the percentages. Compare puts both quotes on one basis — total interest versus total profit, fees included — and shows which one actually costs less.

5  ·  When the lunar year completes — Zakat

When the hawl completes, zakat is due at 2.5% of net zakatable assets — cash, stock, receivables, gold and silver, less payables due within the year — once they reach the nisab. Fixed assets are not zakatable; the working belongs in every business's annual routine.
